## Slide 4 — Dataset

* 768 patients (Pima Indian women aged 21 or older), 8 features + Outcome
* Features: Pregnancies, Glucose, BloodPressure, SkinThickness, Insulin, BMI, DiabetesPedigreeFunction, Age

* Hidden missing values: impossible **zeros**, with Insulin 48.7 % and SkinThickness 29.6 % missing

## Slide 7 — Preprocessing & SMOTE

* Zeros → NaN in 5 medical columns (a rule-based step, so no leakage)
* **Stratified 80/20 split first:** 614 train / 154 test, both about 35 % diabetic
* Pipeline: **median imputation → StandardScaler → SMOTE → model**, fitted on training data only
* SMOTE balances training data from 400/214 to 400/400; **the test set is never resampled**
* Without imbalance handling, LR recall falls to 0.57; SMOTE raises it to 0.72

**Suggested visual:** `11_smote_class_distribution.png` and a small pipeline diagram.

**Speaker notes:**
"The key idea is leakage prevention. I split before fitting anything, so medians, scaling parameters and SMOTE's synthetic samples come only from training data. SMOTE sits inside an imbalanced-learn pipeline, so during cross-validation it only touches the training folds. My quick experiment showed why it matters: without it, Logistic Regression missed over 40 % of diabetic patients."

## Slide 8 — Models Used

* **Logistic Regression:** linear, interpretable baseline
* **K-Nearest Neighbors (k = 15):** similarity-based
* **Decision Tree (depth 5):** human-readable rules
* **Random Forest (300 trees):** bagging ensemble
* **XGBoost (200 trees, depth 3, learning rate 0.05):** boosting ensemble
* Same pipeline for all models, `random_state = 42`, no tuning on the test set

**Suggested visual:** Table of the five models with icons for linear / distance / tree / ensemble.

**Speaker notes:**
"I chose five models from different families, from simple to complex. Scaling is essential for Logistic Regression and KNN; tree models don't need it, but I kept one consistent pipeline. I used sensible fixed settings rather than a big hyperparameter search, which keeps the project explainable and avoids overfitting to a small dataset."

## Slide 9 — Evaluation Metrics

* **Accuracy:** overall correctness, misleading with 65/35 imbalance
* **Precision:** of those flagged, how many are truly diabetic
* **Recall:** of diabetic patients, how many are caught (key for screening)
* **F1:** balance of precision and recall
* **ROC-AUC:** ranking quality across all thresholds
* Protocol: 5-fold CV on training data plus one evaluation on the untouched test set

**Suggested visual:** Annotated confusion-matrix diagram (TP, TN, FP, FN).

**Speaker notes:**
"A model that always says 'no diabetes' gets 65 % accuracy, which shows why accuracy alone is not enough. A false negative means a diabetic patient is missed and may not be referred; a false positive leads to an extra test. So I focus on recall and ROC-AUC, while still checking precision so the model doesn't flag everyone."

## Slide 10 — Results & Model Comparison

| Model (test set) | Acc | Prec | Recall | F1 | AUC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| XGBoost | 0.753 | 0.625 | 0.741 | 0.678 | 0.826 |
| Logistic Regression | 0.708 | 0.574 | 0.648 | 0.609 | 0.811 |
| **Random Forest** | **0.747** | **0.615** | **0.741** | **0.672** | **0.809** |
| KNN | 0.695 | 0.545 | 0.778 | 0.641 | 0.797 |
| Decision Tree | 0.727 | 0.583 | 0.778 | 0.667 | 0.762 |

* Selection rule: recall ≥ 0.70 in **both** CV and test, then the best average of all 5 metrics over CV and test
* **Random Forest selected:** recall 0.741 (40 of 54 diabetic patients caught), CV AUC 0.829
* XGBoost was best on the test set, but its CV recall (0.687) failed the screening rule

**Suggested visual:** `14_roc_curves.png` and `13_confusion_matrices.png` (Random Forest highlighted).

**Speaker notes:**
"No model wins every metric. XGBoost has the best test AUC, but its cross-validated recall is below 0.70. Logistic Regression is best in cross-validation but worst on test recall. Random Forest is consistently first or second in both evaluations and meets the recall requirement, so I chose it. With only 154 test patients, consistency is more trustworthy than one high score."

### Likely viva questions (preparation)

| Question | Short answer |
|---|---|
| Why not just drop rows with zeros? | It would remove about half of the data (Insulin is 48.7 % missing). Median imputation keeps all rows. |
| Why median, not mean? | Insulin and SkinThickness are right-skewed; the median is robust to outliers. |
| Why split before imputation and SMOTE? | Otherwise test-set information leaks into training and the scores become optimistic. |
| Why is SMOTE not applied to the test set? | The test set must reflect the real class distribution of future patients. |
| Why scale for tree models? | It is not needed for the trees themselves, but it keeps one consistent pipeline and gives SMOTE's distance computations comparable feature scales. |
| Why not select XGBoost (best test AUC)? | Its cross-validated recall (0.687) failed the screening requirement, while Random Forest was consistent across CV and test. |
| What does ROC-AUC 0.809 mean? | An 80.9 % chance that a random diabetic patient is ranked above a random non-diabetic patient. |
| Does SHAP prove glucose causes diabetes? | No. It explains how the model uses features, which reflects associations in the data. |
